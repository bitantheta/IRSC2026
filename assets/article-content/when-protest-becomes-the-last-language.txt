## The Cockroach Janata Party Movement and the Demand for Accountability

The Cockroach Janata Party (CJP) had protested in Jantar Mantar, New Delhi about two months approx, followed by the hunger strike performed by the famous educator and environmental activist Sonam Wangchuk. The protest took place because of the repeated mismanagements of competitive exams conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA), followed by the malpractice in examinations and question paper leaks. The recent National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET) paper leak, followed by the suicide of about 21 students amid the paper leak and the canceling of examination stress, this led to the nationwide outrage demanding governmental and ministerial accountability. But rather, listening to the voices and protests the government chose silence in the first place, then after seeing the massive growth of outrage and the continuation of the hunger strike by Sonam Wangchuk the government chose to detain him forcefully by the Delhi Police, from the protest site at Jantar Mantar on the 20th day (entering the 21st day) of his hunger strike. Then the protesting students and individuals marched to the Indian Parliament on July 20, 2026 during the first day of the ongoing monsoon session of the parliament. The “Sansad Chalo” march was organized to demand the government’s accountability for exam paper leaks and the resignation of the education minister for the mismanagement in conducting the examinations. 
The protest witnessed a massive outrage by the Gen-Z groups, also by the of experienced vocal individuals such as famous actor Prakash Raj who stood as an active participant throughout the movement, Shabana Azmi visited the protest site ahead of the march to support the protesting students and address the gathering, activist Yogendra Yadav have shown his active participance throughout the movement, Kunal Kamra, the comedian and political satirist joined the physical protest site in Delhi. The protest and the march was covered by the independent journalists, those brought up the actual news of the ongoing outrage, refraining the governmental faithful mainstream media to mobilize over the movement. The independent media have been covering the CJP protest from the beginning, and kept on delivering the public reactions in an unbiased manner and have connected the movement with every corner of the nation and largely connected with the youths of the nation this is how the independent and unbiased media had contributed to the movement without considering their safety as they were among the major individuals who had faced the police brutality throughout the march to the parliament. The protest made it clear that the running of the present government is lacking the satisfaction of the people, which is ultimately leading to the nationwide outrage. And as an answer to it the government responses with silence, detention of protestors, and the violence by the police. This government tries to corrupt the mind of the citizens, by making complete control over the digital mainstream media, which is accessible by every part, section and class of the society, and people hugely depends on the main stream media as a source of information. The BJP government does not let any institutions to question them. And they did so with the CJP protest, the mainstream media labeled the CJP protest a foreign funded movement, which was apparently dismissed by the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA), the media also labeled Sonam Wangchuk as a Chinese agent, which is among the most disturbing claims. The complete control over the media by the BJP government is one of the foremost reasons that the democracy of India is in an immense threat. No doubt why the Reporters Without Borders (RSF) categorizes the press freedom situation in India as “very serious”, and ranked the Indian media 157th out of 180 countries. The state is said to be stepping towards a new and developed India, but when the needs and voices of the youth are being suppressed by power and brutality, this itself is undermining the very pillars of it.

## The Legacy of Hunger Strikes: Then and Now

In 1984, Sonam Wangyal, a veteran Ladakhi politician, went on a five-day hunger strike demanding Scheduled Tribe (ST) status for several tribal communities in Ladakh. His fast ended after then Prime Minister Indira Gandhi travelled to Leh, met him personally and assured him that the Centre would consider the demand. And when Sonam Wangyal’s son Sonam Wangchuk was on a hunger strike at Jantar Mantar at 2026, he was forcefully detained from the site by the Delhi Police.
Digging to an incident of 2008, when Prof. G.D. Agarwal had performed a hunger strike as his first major protest to save the Ganga, featuring key demands to stop hydroelectric projects, ensure a free-flowing river, and establish a protective committee. This fast had lasted till 18 days and was suspended on 30th June, 2008 after the then government assured to protect the Bhagirathi River. Again Prof. G.D. Agarwal fasted in 2009 for long 38 days, which successfully led to the suspension of the Loharinag Pala Hydroelectric Project. Then comes his final and the most fatal hunger strike in 2018, which lasted will 111 days till his death on 11th October, 2018, but the government did not accept or implement any of his core legislative demands. Here the chronology says that his hunger strikes did hold value, and had led the governmental reforms by listening to his demands and working upon it till the pre–Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) era, and his hunger strike in the BJP era had led to his death and still did not bring any reforms as per his demands. This shows us the absolute stubbornness of the present government which holds no value for the voices and demands of the people. 
The Anna Andolan of 2011 where the social activist Anna Hazare launched an indefinite hunger strike at Ramlila Maidan drawing nationwide public support and global attention, which lasted till 11 days, demanding an independent anti-corruption body with the power to investigate high-ranking public officials, including the prime minister and members of parliament. This movement had forced the then government to pass the Lokpal and the Lokayuktas Act in the parliament. 
This shows that the voices of the people had hold more values in the previous India than this “Acche Din wale India”. The present government rejects the voices that questions, and sticks to its own decisions in a stubborn way, structuring its own benefits, and the benefits of its close Allies. This is where the BJP government differs itself from the previous regimes and proves its governance as a totalitarian rule.
