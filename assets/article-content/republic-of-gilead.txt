THE REPUBLIC OF GILEAD: A DYSTOPIA OR REALITY?
                                -Madhuwrita Nandi (UG-II)
Margaret Atwood’s The Handmaid’s Tale, first published in 1985 is still relevant to this day. Atwood constructed the story from a blend of real-world history, drawing heavily on the 17th century theocratic roots of Puritan New England, 20th century totalitarian regimes, and biblical stories regarding reproduction. The concept of the “Handmaids” who are forced to bear children for the ruling elite is derived directly from the Old Testament. Atwood made a rule when writing the novel that she would only include forms of oppression, control, and subjugation that had already happened at some point in the history of humanity. Therefore, it is no surprise that the Republic of Gilead, as mentioned in the book, is slowly becoming less of a dystopia and more of a reality in the current socio-political situation of the world. 
The Handmaid’s Tale focuses on Offred, who is the protagonist of the story. The entire book is her account of the inception of the Republic of Gilead and its several social norms. She was among the first wave of women recruited for reproductive purposes and allotted to the Commanders, i.e. those who both required such services and could lay claim to them through their position in the elite. The regime created an instant pool of such women by the simple tactic of declaring all second marriages and non-marital relationships adulterous, arresting the women and confiscating the children they already had, who were adopted by childless couples of the upper echelons who were eager for progeny by any means. Men highly placed in the regime were thus able to pick and choose among women who had demonstrated their reproductive fitness by having produced one or more healthy children. The women thus lost their bodily autonomy and were used as properties of the Commanders. They also had to give up their original names. The name “Offred” was composed of the possessive preposition and the first name of the Commander in question. The main focus of the regime was on increasing the population of Gilead since they believed that in pre-Gilead times, birth rates were severely declining. This led to the ban in birth control procedures such as abortion. This is similar to the current situation in the U.S. where in 2022 abortion had been banned by overturning Roe v. Wade, which was a landmark decision of the U.S. Supreme Court in which the court had ruled that the Constitution of the U.S. should protect the right of pregnant women to choose to have an abortion. 
Recently, Katie Miller, the wife of the White House Deputy Chief of Staff for Policy, Stephen Miller, said in a post on X that, “The most radical thing a woman can do is embrace her biological destiny”. She also mentioned that the U.S. is in a “full-scale birth rate collapse” and thus it is women’s biological destiny to have babies and “not slave behind desks chasing careers while our civilization dies”. This is similar to what has been mentioned in The Handmaid’s Tale where The Commander tells Offred that Gilead’s strict rules protect women from being beaten, left alone, or forced to work. He claims that Gilead lets women fulfil their “biological destinies” in peace. On 10th July, the Uttar Pradesh Governor Anandiben Patel said that women should strive to become “expert mothers” before aspiring to become IAS officers or teachers. This emphasis on motherhood and fulfilment of women’s biological destiny treats women as vessels of population growth rather than individuals with their own aspirations and dreams. While there are significant concerns about population decline in countries like the U.S., the problematization of low birth rates is an issue in and of itself since it leads to a decline in women’s rights, female autonomy, and a woman’s right to choose. The focus on the reproductive role of women also marginalizes the Queer community, who are often not only ostracized but also executed, as seen in the book. Margaret Atwood also mentions in the book how women were forced to give up their financial independence and had to depend on their husbands for buying basic necessities such as groceries. This is not far from reality since in the U.S., as well as in most parts of India, women are unable to enjoy their rights and liberties, forcing them to relent themselves to detrimental patriarchal norms. The obsession with “traditional Christian family values” in the U.S. has been on the rise, subjecting women to the tradwife subculture. Same is the case in India where women are expected to prioritise their families instead of their careers. Besides, in India, education for girls is still not treated as a necessity in most parts of the country, especially in rural areas. 
 The Handmaid’s Tale is not a dystopian novel but rather a warning, forcing us to reflect upon the future of humanity. Afterall, the elements of oppression as mentioned in the book are nothing new and have been found across various civilizations and cultures throughout history in order to subjugate women.
